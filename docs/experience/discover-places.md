This is an experience design brief. It does not define screens,
routes, components, APIs or schemas. It describes the intended
member experience of Discover Places so that future product work
can be measured against it.

---

## Purpose

Discover Places helps members understand the landscape of Fresh
Collective.

It is not:

- a map
- a directory
- navigation

It is a way of understanding the world that communities belong to.

Members should leave Discover Places feeling oriented, curious and
inspired.

---

## Core question

Discover Places should quietly answer:

> "What kind of world is this?"

and, later,

> "Where would I like to spend more time?"

The two questions matter in sequence. A member arriving for the
first time is orienting; a member returning is choosing. Discover
Places should serve both.

---

## Philosophy

Places give meaning to Collectives.

A Collective is a community. A Place provides its context.

Every Collective in Fresh Collective belongs somewhere in the
world in two complementary ways:

- Its **Geographic Location** — the real-world city, region or
  Online context in which it operates (Melbourne, Byron Bay,
  Vancouver, Online).
- Its **Collective Home** — the atmosphere, personality and
  visual identity it carries (Sanctuary Springs, Atlas Isles,
  Canopy Reach, The Grove).

Discover Places is a lens on the wider world through the first,
enriched by the second. A member wandering the map encounters not
only *where* communities exist, but the character they carry —
the atmosphere of a Place emerging in part from the Collective
Homes of the communities gathered there.

People often connect with places before they connect with people
— a quiet cabin, a bustling harbour, a peaceful garden, a
creative studio, a mountain trail. These places create
expectations, emotions and stories before someone ever joins a
Collective.

Discover Places should invite exploration rather than simply help
people navigate.

---

## Design principles

### Places tell stories

Every Place should feel like it has its own identity and
character. Members should feel invited *into* somewhere rather
than directed *to* somewhere.

### Context before content

Help members understand *why* a Place exists before showing
everything inside it. The Place is the frame; the Collectives and
Gatherings are what happens within it.

### Geography is emotional

Places are not simply coordinates. Their character emerges from
the communities they hold and the Collective Homes those
communities carry — the same city can feel like one thing through
a *Sanctuary Springs* Collective and another through a *Canopy
Reach* Collective. Discover Places should honour that texture
rather than reduce a Place to its point on a map.

### Discovery should reward curiosity

Members should enjoy wandering through Places without necessarily
looking for anything specific. Unexpected discoveries should feel
natural — as if the world quietly opened another door.

### Places should remain timeless

Avoid making Places feel tied to temporary events. Events happen
*within* Places. Places endure.

### The world should feel coherent

Every Place should contribute to one connected world rather than a
collection of unrelated destinations. A member exploring Byron Bay
and a member exploring Edinburgh should feel they are wandering
different rooms of the same house.

---

## The role of the experience

Discover Places supports experiences such as:

- understanding the different regions of Fresh Collective
- exploring Places connected to particular themes
- discovering online and physical Places
- understanding the atmosphere of different communities
- recognising relationships between Places
- finding Places worth revisiting

---

## What members should notice

The qualities members should naturally perceive when they arrive
at a Place — not statistics, but felt qualities.

### Atmosphere

How a Place feels — shaped in part by the Collective Homes of the
communities gathered there.

### Purpose

Why this Place exists — what draws communities to it.

### Communities

The Collectives that call this Place home.

### Activity

Not simply how busy a Place is, but whether it feels alive.

### Connections

Nearby or related Places that naturally encourage further
exploration.

### Identity

The symbols, artwork and language that make each Place memorable.

---

## Relationship to Collectives

Places provide context. Collectives provide belonging.

A Place may contain many Collectives. Collectives give life to
Places. Neither replaces the other. A Place without Collectives is
a name on a page; a Collective without a Place still exists, but
is not part of Discover Places.

---

## Relationship to Collective Home

Every Collective belongs somewhere in the Fresh Collective world.

Its **Collective Home** provides atmosphere, personality and
visual identity.

Its **Geographic Location** tells members where it operates.

Neither replaces the other. Together they help members understand
both *where* a Collective exists and *what it feels like*.

Discover Places is the surface that brings the geographic layer
into view. Collective Homes travel with the Collectives
themselves — they are what makes one Collective in Melbourne feel
like a mountain refuge and another like a coastal studio, even
when they share the same city on the map. A member exploring a
Place therefore encounters a shared geographic context; the
distinct Collective Homes within that Place give each community
its own felt character.

> *A short architectural note for internal readers.* The Collective
> Home system is implemented as the platform's internal
> world-building framework (referred to in the codebase and admin
> tooling as *the Atlas*). Members and Creators encounter only the
> product term *Collective Home*.

---

## Relationship to Your World

Discover Places introduces the wider world.

Your World reflects the Places that have become personally
meaningful.

A member early in their journey may lean on Discover Places to
form a sense of the world; a member with years of practice may
only visit occasionally, when curiosity strikes. Both patterns are
legitimate.

---

## Relationship to Explore Collectives

Discover Places helps someone understand the world.

Explore Collectives helps them discover communities within that
world.

The two experiences should complement one another rather than
duplicate each other. A member arriving through Discover Places
may follow a Place into its Collectives; a member arriving through
Explore Collectives may follow a Collective back to its Place.
Either path is valid.

---

## What does not belong

Discover Places must not become:

- a navigation menu
- a property listing
- a GIS map
- a directory organised only by geography
- an activity heatmap
- a popularity ranking of Places
- a collection of disconnected destinations

The purpose is to cultivate wonder and orientation, not efficiency
alone.

---

## Success

Someone leaving Discover Places should feel:

- oriented
- curious
- inspired
- connected to the wider world
- excited to explore further

Not:

- lost
- rushed
- overwhelmed
- confused
- as though they simply looked at another menu

If Discover Places consistently produces the second column, the
design has failed regardless of what any metric says.

---

## Future evolution

Discover Places should evolve as the world grows, while keeping
the sense that it is *one* world rather than a fragmented set of
destinations. Considerations for how it may grow:

- **Many regions of the world.** As Fresh Collective expands
  internationally, Discover Places must gracefully hold a world
  that is no longer centred on any one country.
- **Seasonal experiences.** The world may breathe with the year —
  particular Places quieter or louder at particular times, with
  the surface reflecting that rhythm calmly rather than through
  urgency.
- **Evolving world-building.** As curation deepens, individual
  Places acquire richer identity through artwork, language,
  editorial description and cultural texture. As the Collective
  Homes of the Collectives within a Place shift over time, the
  felt character of the Place shifts with them.
- **Deeper interplay between Place and Collective Home.**
  Discover Places may increasingly reveal the atmospheric layer
  alongside the geographic one — a member exploring a Place
  noticing which Collective Homes are strongest there, or a
  member drawn to a particular Collective Home seeing which
  Places it currently lives in — without the two ideas
  collapsing into one.

The invariant across all of this: the world should always feel
coherent, never fragmented. A member should never arrive at a
Place and feel it belongs to a different platform than the one
they left.
